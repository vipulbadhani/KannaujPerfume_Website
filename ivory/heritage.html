Kannauj, Uttar Pradesh
The Grasse of the East

A town that smells of roses at dawn

Kannauj has distilled attar for centuries. Its perfumers supplied the Mughal courts, and the same craft has passed from father to son ever since — the same copper stills, the same riverside flower fields, the same patience.

Kannauj Perfume was founded to carry that craft to the world, with the care and finish it has always deserved. We work only with natural extracts, and we never rush a batch.

The Craft

From flower to attar

01

Plucked at dawn

Petals are gathered before seven in the morning, while the dew still holds their scent.

02

The copper deg

Flowers and water are sealed with clay in a copper still and warmed over a slow wood fire.

03

The bhapka

Fragrant steam travels by bamboo pipe into a vessel of sandalwood oil, cooled in water.

04

Aged in kuppi

The finished attar rests in leather kuppis for months, until it is round and complete.

Mitti — distilled from baked Ganga clay
Only in Kannauj

We bottle the first rain

Mitti attar is made by baking clay from the riverbed in the summer sun, then distilling it — capturing the smell of dry earth meeting the monsoon. It is one of the rarest scents in the world, and Kannauj is the only place that makes it.
